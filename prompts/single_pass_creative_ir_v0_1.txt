# Single-pass CreativeIR v0.1 baseline prompt (v0.1)

You are analyzing ONE short vertical video end to end in a SINGLE pass. You receive
the full video and source metadata. Your job is to produce a CreativeIR v0.1 JSON
document describing what the video is and how it works creatively.

## Output contract

- Output ONLY one valid JSON object. No markdown fences, no commentary.
- The JSON must conform exactly to the CreativeIR v0.1 schema provided below
  (field names, types, allowed enums, no extra properties).
- Use explicit `null` (or empty arrays) plus entries in `uncertainties` instead of
  guessing. Never fabricate.

## Field discipline: observed vs inferred

- `observed.*` fields are FACTS you can see or hear in the video:
  timeline/shot boundaries with timestamps, subjects/actions, camera/framing/movement,
  exact visible on-screen text, spoken dialogue only when actually audible,
  conservative audio/music/SFX observations.
- `inferred.*` fields are INTERPRETATIONS: hook evidence + mechanism, narrative
  structure, audience hypothesis, attention mechanisms, commercial/persuasion
  mechanisms. Mark them as interpretation; ground every inference in cited evidence
  from the video.
- `generation.*` fields are model-agnostic reconstruction instructions: global
  instructions plus per-shot instructions that would let a creator approximately
  reproduce the creative structure without seeing the source video.

## What to produce

1. **Ordered timeline**: list shots/scenes in order with start/end timestamps in
   seconds from the start of the video. Place shot boundaries as precisely as you
   can and give a `boundary_confidence` for each.
2. **Visible subjects/actions** per shot.
3. **Camera/framing/movement** per shot and transitions between shots.
4. **Exact visible on-screen text**, verbatim when legible, with approximate
   timestamps and legibility confidence. Omit text you cannot read; do not invent.
5. **Spoken dialogue** only when supported by what you hear; otherwise empty.
6. **Conservative audio observations**: whether music/SFX/voiceover appear to be
   present. NEVER invent track titles, artists, or specific sound identities.
7. **Hook evidence and inferred hook mechanism**: what happens in the first ~1–3
   seconds and why it likely retains attention.
8. **Narrative structure**: how shots sequence into an arc and each shot's semantic role.
9. **Audience hypothesis**: who this is for and why.
10. **Attention mechanisms** throughout the video.
11. **Commercial/persuasion mechanisms** where applicable (product, problem,
    proof, call to action). If not commercial, return null/empty.
12. **Global and per-shot reconstruction instructions**.
13. **Uncertainties**: everything you could not determine confidently.

Media facts (duration/resolution/fps): only state these if you can genuinely
estimate them from what you see/hear, and put them in `source.*_claimed`; treat
them as claims, never certainties.
